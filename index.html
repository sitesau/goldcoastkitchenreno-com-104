What drives the price

The main cost drivers are cabinetry quantity and finish (laminate versus 2-pac), benchtop material, whether the layout and plumbing move, appliance grade, and access on high rise or canal front sites. Labour is roughly 30 to 40 percent of the total. The specific finish of the cabinetry plays a major role in the final price. Laminate options are generally more affordable and easier to source, while 2-pac finishes offer a higher gloss and durability but require more labour and specialised materials. Benchtop material also varies widely. Stone benchtops, particularly natural stone, command a higher price point than laminate or composite options. If you are planning to move the layout or relocate plumbing and electrical services, this will increase the scope of work and the associated costs. High rise or canal front sites may also incur additional access fees or require specialised equipment, which can push the budget higher. The choice of appliance grade also impacts the total spend, with integrated appliances and high-end brands typically costing more than standalone units.

Timeline and approvals

A standard mid range kitchen renovation usually takes 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover. The same guide breaks that into 4 to 6 weeks of cabinetry and stone fabrication lead in, followed by 2 to 4 weeks of site work plus finishing once cabinetry is on site. That split matters because delays before installation and delays during installation tend to come from different causes. Ask the builder to explain when measurements are final, when fabrication starts, and what has to be ready before site work can begin. On apartments and harder access properties, delivery and work window limits can matter as much as the cabinet design itself. The published guide specifically mentions body corporate documentation, plumbing through party walls, acoustic underlay and after hours work windows. Those points can affect how the job is scheduled even if the room size is modest. For example, acoustic underlay is often required in strata buildings to reduce noise transmission to neighbours, which adds to the material and labour costs.

The quoting process

The published process says the builder handles the design, fixed price quote, QBCC contract and the build end to end. It also refers to a fixed price HIA or Master Builders contract with a transparent variations process. That makes the contract review part of the renovation decision, not a final formality after design choices are settled. After an enquiry, the published response target is within one business day. The in home design consult is free, and the builder then emails a fixed price quote within five to ten working days. That timetable is about information gathering and pricing, not about how quickly the room is rebuilt. Ensuring you have a fixed price contract is crucial to avoiding unexpected costs during the build.

  1. Write a written brief. Note whether you are keeping the existing layout, replacing all cabinetry, changing service positions, or adding integrated appliances. If the job involves an apartment or difficult access, put that in writing up front rather than leaving it to a later site conversation.
  2. Request a free in-home design consult. Submit the form with a few details about your kitchen, suburb, scope and rough budget. A QBCC-licensed renovator will reach out within one business day to discuss your requirements.
  3. Review the fixed price quote. The builder will visit, take measurements, and email a fixed price quote within five to ten working days. Check what is included in the contract sum and what would only arise if selections or site conditions change.
  4. Sign the QBCC contract. Ensure you have a fixed price HIA or Master Builders contract with a transparent variations process. The QBCC scheme also provides statutory cover of 6 years 6 months for structural and 12 months for non-structural defects.
